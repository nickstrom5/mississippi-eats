Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a back road.

What stays on your phone

Mississippi Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Mississippi Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The hot tamale, catfish, po'boy, plate lunch, comeback sauce and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: Visit Mississippi's own pages, the MICHELIN Guide or a dated local story. The wider directory of 7,362 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that matched an inspected business most often in MSDH's records.

Where are the Delta hot tamales?

Our tamale list has 9 hand-checked places, from Doe's Eat Place in Greenville, a James Beard America's Classic, to Hicks' in Clarksdale, Solly's in Vicksburg and Fat Mama's in Natchez.

How do I check a Mississippi restaurant's inspection grade?

The Mississippi State Department of Health grades every inspection A, B or C and posts the grade in the restaurant. The app shows MSDH's latest grade on each place it matches, dated and labeled as MSDH's own; it computes no grade. MSDH's food facility search has every inspection.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. The Department of Revenue's alcohol permits are only searchable, and many counties and towns are dry or partly dry, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
